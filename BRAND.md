trade marks, marked ™. This file records **when each was first used in public
and that use has been continuous since** — the facts a passing-off claim turns
on, written down while they are easy to check rather than years later.

Every date here cites a source that is **dated by someone other than us**.
Git commit dates are shown for context, but they are self-asserted and can be
rewritten, so nothing rests on them alone.

## Worth knowing

- **Unregistered ≠ automatic.** In the UK an unregistered mark is protected by
  passing off, which needs **goodwill** among the relevant public, a
  misrepresentation, and damage — not merely first use. Evidence of use is
  necessary but not sufficient; evidence of recognition is what carries it.
  ("First use" as a source of rights in itself is a US doctrine.)
- **Registration is mostly an operational matter.** Apple's Claims of Right
  form, Google Play, Meta and most platform IP flows ask for a registration
  number, and an unregistered claim tends to be rejected or slow-walked by a
  support queue that cannot weigh goodwill evidence.
- **"MyLLM" is close to descriptive** — "My" plus the generic term for the
  technology — which weakens it both at registration and in a passing-off
  claim. *MyLLMos* and *Opticell* are considerably stronger marks.
- **Someone else is now using the name.** OPTICELL REGENERATIVE HEALTH LIMITED
  was incorporated on 5 July 2026 (Companies House 17321181). Different field,
  so no conflict is expected in software classes — but it is a live reminder
  that company-name availability is not trade-mark protection.

None of the above is legal advice; it is a record of facts and the questions
they raise.
